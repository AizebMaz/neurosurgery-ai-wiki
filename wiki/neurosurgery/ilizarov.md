# Gavriil Ilizarov

Soviet orthopedic surgeon (1921-1992) who developed the principles of [[distraction-osteogenesis|distraction osteogenesis]]. His work on limb lengthening provided the biological foundation for modern [[posterior-calvarial-augmentation|cranial distraction techniques]].

## Key Contributions

### Distraction Osteogenesis Principles
- Gradual, controlled traction on divided bone
- Stimulates new bone formation
- Preserves blood supply
- Allows soft tissue adaptation

### Rate Studies
Ilizarov compared distraction rates:
- **1 mm/day**: Optimal bone formation
- **2 mm/day**: Compromised vascularity
- **>2 mm/day**: Failed bone formation
- **<1 mm/day**: Premature consolidation

## Application to Cranial Surgery

His principles adapted for [[posterior-calvarial-augmentation|PCA]]:
- Rate: 0.5 mm twice daily (1 mm/day)
- Gradual expansion overcomes scalp resistance
- Preserves pericranial blood supply
- Allows neo-osteogenesis
