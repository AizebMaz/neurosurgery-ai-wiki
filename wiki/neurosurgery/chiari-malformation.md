# Chiari Malformation

Hindbrain herniation through the foramen magnum, commonly seen in craniosynostosis due to small posterior fossa volume. Traditionally treated with [[foramen-magnum-decompression|foramen magnum decompression]], but [[posterior-calvarial-augmentation|Posterior Calvarial Augmentation (PCA)]] offers an alternative by addressing the root cause.

## Pathophysiology in Craniosynostosis

- Small posterior fossa (due to cranial deformity)
- Cerebellar tonsils descend through foramen magnum
- CSF flow obstruction
- Syringomyelia may develop

## Types

### Type I
- Cerebellar tonsils descend >5 mm below foramen magnum
- Most common in craniosynostosis
- May be asymptomatic or cause:
  - Headaches
  - Neck pain
  - Balance problems
  - Syringomyelia

### Type II (Arnold-Chiari)
- Cerebellar vermis and brainstem herniated
- Associated with myelomeningocele
- Not typically seen in craniosynostosis

## Treatment Approaches

### Traditional: Foramen Magnum Decompression
- Removes bone at foramen magnum
- Opens dura
- Risks: CSF leak, infection, instability

### Modern: Posterior Calvarial Augmentation
**Mechanism:**
- Increases posterior fossa volume
- Allows cerebellar ascent
- Restores CSF flow
- Syringomyelia deflates

**Advantages:**
- No dural opening
- Lower infection risk
- More physiological
- Addresses root cause (small posterior fossa)

## Outcomes with PCA

- Chiari regression seen as early as 3 months
- Syringomyelia deflation
- Improved CSF flow
- No need for subsequent foramen magnum decompression in most cases
