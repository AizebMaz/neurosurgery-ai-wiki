# Syringomyelia

A fluid-filled cyst (syrinx) within the spinal cord, commonly associated with [[chiari-malformation|Chiari malformation]] in craniosynostosis. Can be effectively treated with [[posterior-calvarial-augmentation|Posterior Calvarial Augmentation (PCA)]] by addressing the underlying posterior fossa crowding.

## Pathophysiology

In craniosynostosis:
1. Small posterior fossa
2. Cerebellar tonsils descend ([[chiari-malformation|Chiari malformation]])
3. CSF flow obstruction at foramen magnum
4. Abnormal CSF pressure dynamics
5. Syrinx formation in spinal cord

## Clinical Presentation

- Neck pain
- Scoliosis
- Motor weakness
- Sensory disturbances
- Pain/temperature dissociation

## Treatment

### Traditional Approach
- [[foramen-magnum-decompression|Foramen magnum decompression]]
- Syrinx shunting
- Addresses symptoms but not root cause

### PCA Approach
**Mechanism:**
- Posterior calvarial expansion increases posterior fossa volume
- Cerebellar tonsils ascend
- CSF flow normalizes
- Syrinx fluid drains spontaneously
- Deflation without shunting

**Timeline:**
- Improvements seen as early as 3 months
- Gradual deflation over time
- MRI confirms resolution

## Advantage of PCA

Unlike traditional approaches:
- No shunt hardware
- No dural opening
- Addresses root cause
- Permanent solution
