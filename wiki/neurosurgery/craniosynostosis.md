# Craniosynostosis

Premature fusion of one or more cranial sutures, resulting in restricted skull growth perpendicular to the affected suture and compensatory growth parallel to it. This creates characteristic skull deformities and, in severe cases, increased intracranial pressure (ICP).

## Types

### Simple (Nonsyndromic)
- Single suture involvement
- Most common:
  - [[sagittal-synostosis|Sagittal synostosis]] (scaphocephaly)
  - [[coronal-synostosis|Coronal synostosis]] (plagiocephaly)
  - [[metopic-synostosis|Metopic synostosis]] (trigonocephaly)

### Complex (Syndromic)
- Multiple sutures involved
- Associated with genetic syndromes:
  - [[crouzon-syndrome|Crouzon syndrome]]
  - [[apert-syndrome|Apert syndrome]]
  - [[pfeiffer-syndrome|Pfeiffer syndrome]]

## Pathophysiology

- Premature suture fusion → restricted growth perpendicular to suture
- Brain continues to grow → compensatory growth parallel to suture
- Results in characteristic deformities
- Severe cases: increased ICP, optic nerve compression, developmental delay

## Surgical Management

### Traditional Approaches
- **Strip craniectomy**: Removal of fused suture
- **Cranial vault remodeling**: Repositioning of cranial bones
- [[foramen-magnum-decompression|Foramen magnum decompression]]: For Chiari malformation

### Modern Innovations
- [[posterior-calvarial-augmentation|Posterior Calvarial Augmentation (PCA)]]: Addresses posterior fossa volume deficiency
- [[endoscopic-suturectomy|Endoscopic suturectomy]]: Minimally invasive option for young infants
- [[distraction-osteogenesis|Distraction osteogenesis]]: Gradual expansion

## Indications for Posterior Intervention

Specific scenarios requiring posterior approaches:

1. **Brachyturricephaly** (tall, short skull)
2. **Pansynostosis** (all sutures fused)
3. **Lambdoid synostosis** (posterior plagiocephaly)
4. **Chiari malformation** with small posterior fossa
5. **Syringomyelia** secondary to hindbrain herniation
