# Posterior Calvarial Augmentation (PCA)

A surgical technique for treating craniosynostosis with posterior calvarial deficiency, developed by [[guirish-solanki|Guirish A. Solanki]] and colleagues. The procedure augments the posterior skull vault to increase intracranial volume and relieve compression on the brain and hindbrain structures.

## Philosophy

> "If the problem is at the back of the head, operate at the back of the head."
> — Tony Hockley, 2008

Rather than performing [[foramen-magnum-decompression|foramen magnum decompression]] for [[chiari-malformation|Chiari malformation]] in craniosynostosis, PCA addresses the root cause: insufficient posterior fossa volume.

## Two Approaches

| Approach | Technique | Timeline | Best For |
|----------|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Fixed PCA** | Single-stage, resorbable plates | Immediate | Older children/adults; where distraction unavailable |
| **Dynamic PCA (PCD)** | [[distraction-osteogenesis|Distraction osteogenesis]] over 2-3 weeks | Latency → Distraction → Consolidation | Infants/toddlers; tight scalp |

## Indications

Patients who benefit from PCA include:

1. **Syndromic craniosynostosis** with raised ICP and severe deformity (brachyturricephaly, pansynostosis with microcephaly)
2. **Nonsyndromic craniosynostosis** with pansynostosis or lambdoid synostosis
3. **Craniosynostosis with Chiari** and/or syringomyelia
4. **Tight foramen magnum** with/without central apnea

## Key Advantages

- Much greater increase in intracranial volume vs. traditional approaches
- More physiological positioning of intracranial structures
- Improved brainstem alignment
- Reduced functional aqueduct compression
- Improved CSF flow and reduced venous hypertension
- Regression of hindbrain hernia without foramen magnum decompression
- Deflation of syringomyelia

## The Three Phases (Dynamic PCA)

### 1. Latency Phase
- Post-surgical inflammatory response
- Hematoma formation with cellular building blocks
- Duration: ~5 days in young children

### 2. Distraction Phase
- Rate: 0.5 mm twice daily (1 mm/day total)
- Vector: 0-5° superior to Frankfurt plane
- Duration: 18-30 days
- Parents perform at home after training

### 3. Consolidation Phase
- Neo-osteogenesis in distraction gap
- Duration: ~1 month per 10 mm expansion
- Distractors removed under GA

## Surgical Overview

### Positioning
- Prone position
- Mayfield headrest
- Mark swollen veins to avoid (in venous hypertension cases)

### Craniotomy
- Zig-zag (stealth) bicoronal incision
- Careful preservation of pericranium
- Dura NOT elevated from bone
- Avoid transverse sinus injury

### Distractor Placement
- One distractor per side
- Internal titanium single vector
- 6-9 screws for stability (evolved from 3)
- Flat plates on lower parietal region

## Complications

| Complication | Prevention/Management |
|--------------|----------------------|
| CSF leak | Careful dural preservation |
| Screw loosening | 6-9 screws vs. 3; adequate bone depth |
| Wound breakdown | Gradual distraction; tension-free closure |
| "Gull Winging" | Plating across lambdoid sutures |
| Shunt malfunction | Contraindication: posterior VP shunt |

## Historical Development

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-------------|
| 1996 | First posterior calvarial release (Birmingham) |
| 2003 | "Fixed" larger PCA with resorbable plates (GOSH) |
| 2006 | PCD technique pioneered |
| 2009 | Initial series published |
| Present | Standard procedure for suitable cases |

## Outcomes

Improvements seen as early as 3 months postoperatively:
- Resolution of papilledema
- Ventricular morphology normalization
- Chiari regression
- Syringomyelia deflation
- Scoliosis improvement
