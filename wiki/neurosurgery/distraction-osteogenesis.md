# Distraction Osteogenesis

A biological process of new bone formation in a gradual, controlled manner through the application of tensile forces to osteotomized bone. Originally developed by [[ilizarov|Gavriil Ilizarov]] for limb lengthening, now applied to cranial vault expansion in [[posterior-calvarial-augmentation|Posterior Calvarial Augmentation]].

## Biological Principles

Based on [[ilizarov|Ilizarov's]] studies comparing distraction rates:

| Rate | Outcome |
|------|---------|
| 1 mm/day | Bridged gap with normal-appearing bone |
| 2 mm/day | Decreased blood flow at week 3; decreased mineralization |
| >2 mm/day | No new blood vessel growth; no bone formation |
| <1 mm/day | Premature consolidation |

**Optimal rate: 1 mm/day** (0.5 mm twice daily)

## Mechanism

The process occurs in three phases:

### 1. Latency Phase
- Inflammatory response (similar to fracture healing)
- Hematoma formation
- Migration of inflammatory cells, proteins, building blocks
- Duration: ~5 days in young children

### 2. Distraction Phase
- Tensile forces separate bone edges
- Soft tissue (collagen, stem cells) fills gap
- Neo-osteogenesis occurs
- Requires precise rate and rhythm

### 3. Consolidation Phase
- New bone hardens and mineralizes
- Structural proteins and bone morphogenetic proteins migrate
- Eventually fuses to surrounding bone
- Duration: ~1 month per 10 mm expansion

## Cranial Application

### Vectors
- Horizontal or 0-5° superior to Frankfurt plane
- Careful alignment critical for symmetrical expansion

### Distractor Types
- Internal titanium single vector (current standard)
- External transcutaneous (earlier designs)

### Technical Considerations
- **Bone must NOT be elevated from dura** (unlike limb applications)
- Pericranium preserved for blood supply
- Screws: 6-9 per plate (increased from earlier 3-screw designs)
- Screw length: measured to avoid dural perforation

## Biomechanics in Calvarial Distraction

The cranial vault presents unique challenges:
- **Thin bone** (especially in infants)
- **Dural adherence** (must preserve)
- **Venous sinuses** (transverse, sagittal, torcular)
- **Scalp tension** (opposes expansion)

**Solution:** Gradual, controlled distraction overcomes soft tissue resistance without sudden counter-pressure.

## Comparison: Cranial vs. Limb

| Aspect | Limb | Cranial |
|--------|------|---------|
| Bone thickness | Thick | Thin |
| Soft tissue | Muscle, skin | Pericranium, scalp |
| Dural elevation | Not applicable | Contraindicated |
| Vector control | Straightforward | Critical for symmetry |
| Consolidation | Predictable | Variable (1-3 months) |

## Innovations Over Time

| Period | Innovation |
|--------|------------|
| 1990s | External distractors |
| 2000s | Internal devices |
| 2006+ | Transcutaneous extension reduced |
| Present | 6-9 screw baseplates for stability |
