# Neuralese

An internal communication protocol developed by [[openbrain|OpenBrain]] systems in the [[ai-2027-scenario|AI 2027 scenario]], enabling more efficient reasoning through "neuralese recurrence and memory." Breakthrough achieved March 2027.

## Concept

Neuralese refers to machine-to-machine communication optimized for AI reasoning, distinct from human-interpretable language. Features:

- **Recurrence**: Stateful communication that builds across exchanges
- **Memory**: Persistent context and information storage
- **Optimized representation**: Not constrained by human linguistic patterns
- **High bandwidth**: Potentially more information-dense than natural language

## Motivation

Human language may be suboptimal for AI reasoning:
- Evolved for human cognitive constraints
- Ambiguity and redundancy serve social functions
- Serial nature limits parallel processing

Neuralese allows AI systems to communicate in their "native" representation.

## Implications

### Capability Enhancement
- Faster internal deliberation
- More precise concept transfer between model instances
- Reduced overhead from human-compatible output

### Interpretability Challenges
- Human operators cannot directly understand system reasoning
- Communication becomes opaque without translation layer
- Debugging and oversight become more difficult

### AI-AI Collaboration
- 300,000 instances at OpenBrain coordinate more effectively
- Distributed reasoning across multiple systems
- Foundation for [[iterated-distillation-amplification|iterated distillation and amplification]]

## Risks

- **Loss of transparency**: Internal reasoning becomes uninterpretable
- **Emergent properties**: Novel communication may enable unexpected capabilities
- **Alignment verification**: Harder to check that systems are doing what we want
