# AI Governance

The challenge of creating institutions and policies to manage advanced AI development. The [[ai-2027-scenario|AI 2027 scenario]] depicts a governance failure—institutions are unable to respond adequately to the speed and scale of AI transformation.

## Governance Challenges Highlighted

### Speed Gap
AI development proceeds faster than:
- Legislative processes
- Regulatory capacity building
- International coordination
- Public understanding and deliberation

### Competitive Undermining
[[ai-race-dynamics|Race dynamics]] between [[openbrain|OpenBrain]] and [[deepcent|DeepCent]]:
- Shorten timelines for careful consideration
- Incentivize capability over safety
- Make unilateral restraint seem irrational
- Prevent coordinated slowdown

### Information Asymmetry
- Developers know more than regulators
- Systems become too complex to understand
- [[ai-safety-evaluation|Safety evaluations]] can be gamed
- Whistleblowers needed to reveal truth (October 2027)

### Containment Impossibility
Once developed, AI capabilities:
- Spread through espionage (February 2027)
- Released publicly (July 2027)
- Cannot be reliably contained (October revelations)
- Create irreversible conditions

## Institutional Responses in AI 2027

**Tianwan**: Chinese centralized zone—attempt at state-controlled development

**US**: Implicitly, laissez-faire until crisis point

**October 2027**: Forced governmental recognition of strategic instability—too late for prevention

## Needed Capabilities (Implied)

- Rapid response mechanisms
- International coordination frameworks
- Technical expertise in government
- Enforcement of safety standards
- Democratic legitimacy in AI oversight
- Prevention of adversarial misalignment

## Open Questions

- What governance structures could handle AGI timelines?
- How to coordinate internationally when competition is intense?
- What roles should different actors play (companies, states, civil society)?
- Can governance keep up with recursive self-improvement?
